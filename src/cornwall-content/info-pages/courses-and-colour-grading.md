---
title: Courses and colour grading
summary: How orienteering courses are graded by length and difficulty, from White (very easy) to Black (very hard).
section: participation
order: 1
---

Courses are graded by length and technical difficulty, and identified by a
colour. The courses on offer depend on the venue and the likely experience of
the people taking part. A youngster usually starts on White or Yellow; an adult
newcomer on Yellow or Orange.

| Colour | Who it suits |
| ------ | ------------ |
| **White** (very easy) | All controls on paths. Great for 6-10 year olds and family groups. |
| **Yellow** (easy) | Simple line features such as paths, walls and streams. Under-12s and families. |
| **Orange** (medium) | Introduces basic compass work and route choice. Ideal for novice adults or improving youngsters. |
| **Light Green** | A step up in technical difficulty, using simple contours and point features. Good for improvers. |
| **Green** | Short but navigationally hard. Experienced under-18s and adults wanting a challenge. |
| **Blue** | Longer and more physical than Green, for experienced orienteers. |
| **Brown / Black** | Long, physically demanding and navigationally hard. Experienced orienteers only. |

You can progress either onto longer courses at the same difficulty, or onto
courses with harder navigation, as your confidence grows.
