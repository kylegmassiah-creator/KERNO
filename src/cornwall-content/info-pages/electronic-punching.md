title: Electronic punching (SI)
summary: How electronic timing works at our events, using a SportIdent dibber to record each control.

---

To prove you visited each control in the right order, you "punch" using an
electronic timing chip, usually called a dibber. KERNO uses the SportIdent (SI)
system: each control has a small box with a hole, and you dib your chip into the
hole until it beeps.

The dibber is the size of a finger and clips on with an elastic band. It records
your time at each control, so afterwards you can see your "splits" and compare
your route choices with others.

At the start you "clear" and "check" the dibber, then dib the start box to begin
timing and collect your map. At the finish you dib the finish box to stop the
clock, then go to download, where your result is read off and printed.

You can buy your own dibber (roughly £30-£60), but it's only worth it once you
orienteer regularly, as KERNO loans them at events for about £1.

**The golden rule:** always report to download or the finish, even if you don't
complete the course, so officials know everyone is safely back.
