---
title: Reading the map
summary: What the colours, scales and symbols on an orienteering map mean.
section: participation

Orienteering maps are far more detailed than ordinary maps and use an
internationally agreed set of symbols, drawn to magnetic north. Common scales
are 1:10000 or 1:15000 for terrain, and 1:4000 or 1:3000 for parks and urban
areas. The colours are part of the language of the map:

- **Black** - paths, tracks, fences, buildings, rock features.
- **Brown** - landform: contours, gullies, pits and small hills.
- **Blue** - water: lakes, ponds, streams and marshes.
- **Green** - vegetation density; darker green is harder to run through.
- **White** - runnable woodland.
- **Yellow** - open ground; solid yellow for grass, paler for rough open such as heather.

The triangle marks the start, numbered circles are the controls in order, and a
double circle is the finish. You don't need to memorise the symbols, you'll pick
them up quickly, and most maps include a legend.

A full guide to the control description symbols is available from
[British Orienteering](https://www.britishorienteering.org.uk/).
